Hey there, great to have you back! Today I am gonna tell you about some of our days in the jungle, so sit back, get something to eat or drink and dig in. 

In the evening after Babets story, we went to a Warung nearby, had some traditional indonesian food and after rented a scooter. You can apparently rent a scooter here without a driver license as long as you provide the cash, they never even looked at mine or whether I wrote the license number correctly.

The next day I got sick with Bali belly as well. So that day was for the bin. I had a crazy headache as well even with paracetamol and enough water and ORS. I basically slept all day, while Babet also stayed at the Villa sunbathing and going on a sidequest for some groceries. Living in the jungle has its upsides but also downsides, one of which is that there is no good supermarket closeby and that you can only get here by car or scooter and the grab drivers dont really want to come out here to pick us up because they dont get paid enough for the detour. But Babet did manage to convince a Grab driver to come by the villa and so she got the job done.

On monday I started to feel better with some occasional cramp in the stomach we had a chill morning with staying at the villa swimming, sunbathing and watching all the animals that live here. We have cicadas that make crazy loud sounds, many tropical birds that whizz by, salamanders, giant ants, a rat, many different butterfly, frogs and other insects, also mosquitos. The balinese people have a tradition where they leave some of their own food just outside of their house for the animals and say that this way they stay outside, well that is definitely not the case for the animals near our villa also with such donations visible on out porch. After lunch we went to two waterfalls with the scooter. It was about 35 minutes by scooter to the first one and I have to say driving the scooter in Bali is something else. You have to be fully concentrated at all times because a lot of dogs just sit/walk/sleep on the main roads, people leave giant piles of dirt on the road when they are rebuilding their homes, others just park their cars/scooters or just drive on the wrong side of the road. Also everyone drives very fast on their scooters without helmets, like 70-100 km/h fast which is a speed I did not really want to touch even with a helmet on. Next to everything on the road, the quality of the road itself was also questionable a lot of times and you alsways needed to check for potholes which was especially hard at night. The first waterfall we visited was a bit underwhelming, it was nice and for some extra cash you could get a picture with an asian rice field worker hat. But we did not do that and rather wanted a waterfall where we could also swim to cool down. The second waterfall had a pool section at the bottom and a larger waterfall at the top and it was really refreshing to take a bath there. It is also nice that we came in off-season, only 3 other people were there and it looked like it could take a lot more during prime season as they had lockers and changing rooms ready for large groups of tourists. After we went to a nice restaurant for dinner but it started to rain during our drive there. When we arrived the rain was still light and we did not expect what we had maneuvered ourselves into. Right after starting our dinner a really heavy tropical thunderstorm started to break loose. The hardest rain I had ever seen and lightning probably under 50m because the lightning and sound were almost instant. The main problem was that the restaurant was right at the center of the storm and our villa was actually just outside of the storm, eventhough it was only 25 minutes away by scooter. We did not want to drive in the storm because we were not equipped with anything against rain and it was quite cold to drive wet on a scooter. So we waited two hours at the restaurant at which point the rain started to be a little less so we could try to leave. Driving back was quite something. There were little rivers forming on the street where the rice canals could not transport all of the water anymore and it was quite scary and cold to drive but we managed to get home safe. So quite an eventful first real day to spend in the jungle.

The next day we booked an e-bike tour with the company at which our airbnb host works and het got to show us around as our guide. Also here it was nice that we went off-season as we got basically a private tour with a guide, a personal photographer and another employee shadowing us with a car in case a battery was empty or we needed a new bike. The bikes themselves were self build, we would call it ''gebeund'' in dutch. They took old gravelbikes and put an electric motor to the back gear and added their own batteries to the front and hooked it all up. With your left thumb you could then control how much power you wanted to feed to the gears. Not really fancy and it made a lot of weird noises, but cool that they built it themselves. The trip was 20km long with stops at rice fields, temples and a local home complex where we learned a lot about balinese traditions. The bike being electrical was a necessity with the steep inclines we faced where the motor at full power wasnt even enough to push us up and we also needed to deliver power ourselves as best as we could. Rice is what the locals eat here every day for breakfast, lunch and dinner so it is very important for them and a lot of people still have one rice field per family that they farm on. When marrying they also look at the size of the other families rice field to see whether they are rich or not. One of the traditions is also that the rice field needs to be split up over all boys upon inheritance. Basically everything here revolves around rice apparently. Awan, our airbnb host, explained that their religion is a combination of hinduism and bhuddism and that they also borrow other traditions such as japanese and he explained it this way: We use hinduism as the base but not too strict, strict is no good. And then we add all the good ideas of other religions and traditions and we end up with the way of life of balinese people. Some of the most important concepts include rebirth and karma but also balancing life so ying-yang and they dont enforce the cast system which is a big concept in hinduism because they think its not a good concept of hinduism. I think its really refreshing that people have at least thought through some of the systems certain religions fore upon their people but at the same time the balinese also have some things they basically force themselves to do. Awan always said you can or cant do all of the offerings and or the cleaning your soul every month at a spring closeby but everyone that is a real beliver does these things a lot. But in general I can say that the people here are super friendly and happy and that their way of life is truly inspiring. After the bike tour we went to bed early because the next day we will hike up mount batur (an active volcano) during sunrise and we need to wake up at 2 am. 

We actually slept 5 hours before waking up at 2 am, and the 1 hour drive was alright up to base camp where we would meet our guide cooper. He actually slept less than us, 2 hours, because his friend was just married and they partied all night. Cooper was 24 years old and a pretty chill guide but you could tell that he had some sleep depravation. After 2 hours òf hiking we reached the top with time to spare and got a cup of coffee and some breakfast as the sun was rising. The views were breathtaking and the whole endeavor was definitely worth it. After sunrise we walked along the crater and looked at some of the hot steam that was coming up from the volcano. We also tried to find the monkeys that usually inhabit the volcano but cooper suggested they also had a wedding yesterday and were hungover as well. He showed us his village from the top of the vulcano and explained that all around the vulcano spring onion is being farmed. From above we could also see that there were two more gigantic craters that come from big explosions in ancient times that had formed a lake and the hilly sourrounding of the vulcano. After a while we descended again and went home where we relaxed a bit and took a nap. The rest of the day was quite lazy, chilling at the villa and ordering some food via grab.
