We had some more fun adventures during our last few days in Tegallalang. So settle in again for our latest stories!

On Thursday, we headed from Tegallalang to Ubud again. We started with a coffee tasting on the way. It was really interesting, because they had a whole garden full of spices and fruit that they also mixed into the coffees. They showed us how the coffee was roasted and ground, and there were a few luwaks here too, so you could try luwak coffee as well.

The tasting included no fewer than 17 different coffees and teas. We also ordered an extra luwak coffee so we could compare it properly with a regular coffee. Well, once again we couldn't taste much difference, but the luwak coffee did have a better aftertaste! All the other coffees and teas were mixed with things like vanilla, cocoa and even durian (it was actually quite OK). Unfortunately, everything also contained milk and lots of sugar, so it all tasted incredibly sweet to us. Not quite our thing, but still a fun experience!

We'd booked a cooking class in Ubud afterwards, but still had an hour and a half to spare. So what were we going to do? Head to the Monkey Forest, of course! Off we went on the scooter through Ubud, and let me tell you: people drive like absolute maniacs there. Luckily, we arrived in one piece, although Niels had used up all his energy on the ride and didn't have much left in him. That was fine, though, because I could happily watch the monkeys (and luckily they weren't aggressive). We managed to take a few nice photos before heading to the cooking class.

There, we made a chicken and mushroom soup as a starter, nasi campur for the main course and dadar gulung for dessert. The fun thing was that Anna had already recommended trying dadar gulung, and now we got to make it ourselves! This involved flipping little pancakes, which, as Dutch people, we naturally thought we'd be good at. Well, I think it was the pan's fault…

After cooking, we got to eat everything we'd made, then headed back to the jungle for some lovely downtime.

Before bed, Niels and I watched an episode of Alone (a pretty good series, by the way), and then the fun began. We'd been finding little droppings in the cottage all week, so we knew there must be a small lizard somewhere. Well, while we were watching Alone, something black suddenly ran across me, and sure enough, there was the lizard! We tried to catch it, and Niels instinctively went for its tail. Of course, it immediately dropped its tail. Eventually, we caught the lizard and put it outside. And what was still happily wriggling around in bed? That tail… Once everything (including the tail) was back outside, we could finish watching our series in peace and go to bed.

The next day, we'd booked a quad bike tour on Marieke's recommendation. Well, that really was an experience! By the end of the tour, I think I was covered in mud from head to toe, and about five litres of water came out of my boots. But the tour was absolutely brilliant and is definitely in our top three things we've done on Bali!

After the quad biking, we went home, returned the scooter and relaxed at the cottage, as it was already our last day there. We packed our backpacks again, because the next day, Saturday, we were heading to Gili Trawangan. We'd already booked the boat and had also found a Grab driver who was happy to take us to the harbour. Perfect!

On Saturday morning, the Grab driver arrived at the cottage at nine on the dot. The drive was supposed to take an hour and a half, and we thought we needed the twelve o'clock boat. A little time to spare, but that's how we like it. Then, in the car, we realised the boat wasn't leaving until one. Meanwhile, our Grab driver was driving insanely fast and overtaking every car and scooter he possibly could. Even when there were another fifteen cars ahead of him. I reckon he honked about a hundred times, so the hour-and-a-half drive was over rather sooner than expected.

In the end, we arrived at the harbour with loads of time to spare and enjoyed a lovely cup of coffee there. We also received a beautiful necklace from a local woman we'd had a nice chat with (we'd told her we were from the Netherlands).

We'd chosen the big, expensive boat in the hope that it would be a bit more stable and that we (read: Niels) wouldn't get seasick. Unfortunately, that didn't quite work out, because apparently even a big boat can rock around quite a bit on the waves. Luckily, the boat trip only took two hours, we survived and we've now arrived on Gili T!

Bring on island life!
